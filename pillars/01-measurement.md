# Pillar 1: Measurement

## Central question

**What actually happened?**

Measurement is the foundation of every later model. Bad definitions, denominators, labels, or context adjustments cannot be rescued by sophisticated modeling.

## Core ideas

- define events and outcomes precisely
- choose valid denominators
- distinguish rate, efficiency, volume, and opportunity
- adjust for opponent and context when needed
- quantify reliability and uncertainty
- separate descriptive metrics from latent-skill estimates

## Sports examples

Beach volleyball:
- attack efficiency
- earned sideout percentage
- serve point-scoring percentage
- transition opportunity rate

Ice hockey:
- shot attempts
- expected goals
- controlled-entry rate
- zone-exit success
- on-ice goal share

## Explain → Build → Apply → Defend

**Explain:** Describe why a metric is constructed as it is and what it does not measure.

**Build:** Produce a reproducible metric from event-level data.

**Apply:** Use the metric to answer a real performance question with appropriate filters and context.

**Defend:** Justify the denominator, inclusion rules, opponent/context adjustments, reliability threshold, and interpretation.

## Key warning

A metric can be mathematically correct and still answer the wrong question.
